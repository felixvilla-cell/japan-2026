Day1

Land & Ease In

Land at Narita + Shinjuku by night · Sun Aug 2

Wheels up from O'Hare Saturday morning, land at Narita 2:10 PM Sunday. Drop the bags in Shinjuku, then one rule: stay awake until dark and let the neon do the work.

Locked inUA2373 ORD 6:00 AM → LAX, then UA32 LAX 10:55 AM → Narita, landing Sun Aug 2 at 2:10 PM. Hotel Gracery Shinjuku, four nights.
✦ Felix’s Remix

Jetlag but Make It Neon

Ease-in night, but every stop is a gamer/anime pin instead of just wandering.

  • A multi-floor Sega-lineage arcade a minute from Omoide Yokocho, stacked with UFO catcher crane games, rhythm cabinets like maimai and Chunithm, and fighting-game setups. You feed 100-yen coins in, chase a prize plush, and get your first jetlagged taste of Japanese arcade culture without needing to think hard. Ground floors are all claw machines, upper floors get serious with music and versus games.

    Quick facts

    • Games ~100-200 yen per play
    • Open late, often past midnight
    • Near Seibu-Shinjuku / Shinjuku Station
    • IC card or coins accepted

  • A life-size Godzilla head juts out over the Toho Cinemas building in the heart of Kabukicho and roars with lights and smoke on the hour after dark. It is free, gloriously stupid, and the definitive first-night Tokyo photo. Stand in the plaza below and look straight up; the 8th-floor Godzilla terrace has been closed to visitors since April 2026, so the street is the shot.

    Quick facts

    • Free to view from street
    • Roars hourly after sunset
    • Kabukicho, above Toho Cinemas
    • Best shot ~8-9pm
    • 8F terrace closed since Apr 2026

  • An open-air rooftop observation deck 229 meters up over Shibuya Crossing, with a glass-edge sky corner and hammocks under the stars. The night slot delivers the full sea-of-neon skyline with Tokyo Tower and Skytree glowing in the distance. Book the roughly 9pm timed entry online in advance because sunset slots sell out fast.

    Quick facts

    • Around 2,500 yen adult
    • Book timed slot online early
    • Atop Shibuya Scramble Square
    • Rooftop closes in bad weather

  • The karaoke building made famous by Lost in Translation, where you get a private soundproof room instead of a public stage. The song books are loaded with anime openings, game themes, and Western hits, and no drinking is required to have fun. It is the perfect low-pressure ice-breaker for dad and son after a long flight.

    Quick facts

    • Around 1,000-2,000 yen per person/hour
    • Private room, no strangers
    • Steps from Shibuya Crossing
    • Rooms 601/602 are the movie ones
Keep it light · then crash
Day2

The Tokyo From the Movies

Shibuya + Harajuku · Mon Aug 3

The young, loud, high-energy side of the city — from a quiet forest shrine straight into the chaos.

✦ Felix’s Remix

Shibuya Player Select: Sneakers, Nintendo, Streetwear

Keep Meiji Shrine calm, then turn Shibuya/Harajuku into a flagship-store and thrift crawl aimed at a gamer who dresses.

  • The roof is cut off the top deck, so you sit up in the open air with a smartphone audio guide in your ear and watch the city assemble itself. Three routes all meet at the Marunouchi hub by Tokyo Station: Green runs Shinjuku and Shibuya, Red runs Asakusa and Tokyo Skytree, Blue runs Tokyo Tower, Tsukiji and Ginza. One ticket rides all three as many times as you want, so the move is to loop first for orientation, then hop off and do the day on foot. Buy the two-day ticket and Tuesday's Asakusa and Skytree day rides the Red Route for free.

    Quick facts

    • 1-day 4,800 yen, 2-day 7,000 yen (adult)
    • Three routes, all meet at Marunouchi Mitsubishi Building
    • Runs roughly 9:00 to 19:00, every 20 to 45 min
    • Green Route (Shinjuku/Shibuya) 9:20 to 16:40
    • Audio guide in 7 languages on your own phone
    • Walk-up, no reservation needed

  • Japan's only official Nintendo store sits on the 6th floor of Shibuya Parco alongside a Pokemon Center and a Capcom Store. You get exclusive Mario, Zelda, and Splatoon merch, giant photo installations, and Street Fighter goods all on one floor. For a gamer this floor is basically a pilgrimage.

    Quick facts

    • Free entry, expect a queue
    • Parco 6th floor, Shibuya
    • Around 10 min from Shibuya Station
    • Weekday mornings are quieter

  • The official Shonen Jump store stocked with One Piece, Dragon Ball, Demon Slayer, and Jujutsu Kaisen merch you cannot buy back home. Expect exclusive figures, apparel, and manga-tie-in goods across every big franchise. A must-stop if he grew up on Jump anime.

    Quick facts

    • Free to browse
    • Located around Shibuya area
    • Cash and cards accepted
    • Look for shop-exclusive items

  • The back-alleys behind Harajuku's main drag, where BAPE, atmos, WEGO, Kinji vintage, and Kailua cluster along Cat Street. This is where actual Tokyo teens shop for streetwear and sneakers, not the tourist candy of Takeshita Street. Budget a couple of hours to dig through racks and hunt limited kicks.

    Quick facts

    • Free to wander
    • Between Harajuku and Shibuya
    • Most shops open ~11am-8pm
    • Vintage prices vary widely

  • Two train stops from Shibuya, Shimokita is Tokyo's vintage-clothing capital, threaded with tiny record shops, retro game stores, and mellow cafes. The vibe is low-key and creative, all narrow lanes and secondhand racks. Add it if he is into thrifting streetwear or hunting cheap records.

    Quick facts

    • Free to explore
    • ~3 min from Shibuya by Keio line
    • Bring cash for small shops
    • Best on a slow afternoon

Day3

Old Tokyo, Then Arcade Heaven

Asakusa + Skytree + Akihabara · Tue Aug 4
FELIX'S FAVORITE

A temple from the 600s at sunrise, a 634m tower by noon — then whole buildings that are nothing but arcades, wrapped in anime billboards. Old Tokyo and Electric Town in one loaded day.

✦ Felix’s Remix

Akihabara: Hard Mode

His favorite day is already right; this stacks the deep cuts so it goes from tourist Akiba to real Akiba, plus a night session.

  • The Red Route runs the Asakusa and Tokyo Skytree loop, so a two-day ticket bought Monday morning covers today at no extra cost. Ride it out from the Marunouchi hub for the view from the open top deck, hop off at Asakusa for Senso-ji, and pick the bus back up when you are done rather than working out a train.

    Quick facts

    • Covered by the 2-day ticket (7,000 yen)
    • Red Route = Asakusa and Tokyo Skytree
    • Hop off and back on as many times as you want
    • Buses roughly every 20 to 45 min

  • A guided go-kart tour on actual public streets, cruising past Senso-ji temple and along the Sumida River in a costume of your choice. Dad races son through real Tokyo traffic while a guide leads and GoPro footage gets captured for you. It is the single most unbelievable I-cannot-believe-this-is-legal thing on the trip.

    Quick facts

    • Around 8,000-10,000 yen per person
    • Valid IDP driving permit required
    • Book 1-2 hour tour ahead
    • Costumes provided free

  • A retro shopping complex four floors deep in Mandarake stores selling vintage figures, retro Famicom carts, anime cels, and rare trading cards. It is grittier, cheaper, and far less touristy than Akihabara, aimed at real collectors. A quick JR ride from Shinjuku and an easy swap for the pricey Skytree deck.

    Quick facts

    • Free entry, pay to browse
    • About 5 min from Nakano Station
    • Many shops open from noon
    • Cash preferred at small stalls

  • If you keep Skytree on the plan, skip the expensive observation deck and hit the character shops at its base instead. The Tokyo Solamachi mall houses a Pokemon Center packed with plush and exclusives plus other Nintendo and anime goods stores. You get the Skytree photo from the ground and spend the money on merch.

    Quick facts

    • Shops free to enter
    • At base of Tokyo Skytree
    • Tobu Skytree Station adjacent
    • Skip the ~3,100 yen deck ticket

  • A hands-on museum where you wear real-weight armor, throw ninja stars at targets, and swing a katana in a short guided lesson. It leans cheesy but in the most fun way and sits a short walk from Senso-ji. The photo set of dad and son in full samurai gear basically sells itself.

    Quick facts

    • Around 2,000-3,500 yen with experience
    • Allow about 1 hour
    • Walkable from Asakusa Station
    • Book time slot online

  • A legendary hardcore Akihabara arcade famous for its floor of vertical shoot-em-ups plus Street Fighter and Guilty Gear cabinets full of serious local players. This is where the real Akiba fighting-game and shmup scene lives, not the tourist claw floors. Watch a top player run a bullet-hell board and you will get why it is a landmark.

    Quick facts

    • Games ~100 yen per play
    • Near Akihabara Station
    • Serious players, watch and learn
    • Open late into evening

  • Mandarake Complex packs eight floors of used figures, manga, cels, and retro game carts, while nearby Radio Kaikan is a tower of figure and model shops. Together they are the retro-hunting endgame, going far deeper than Super Potato alone. Give it a couple of hours and expect to find something you have never seen for sale.

    Quick facts

    • Free entry, pay per find
    • Both near Akihabara Station
    • Mandarake is 8 floors
    • Prices climb for rare items

  • Yodobashi Akiba is a nine-floor electronics, gunpla, and game monster where you can lose hours in the model kit and gaming floors. BEEP nearby is a basement shrine to retro PCs, dojin games, and oddball vintage hardware. One is a mega-store, the other a deep-cut collector cave, and both are steps from the station.

    Quick facts

    • Free to browse
    • Yodobashi is 9 floors
    • Right by Akihabara Station
    • Tax-free counter for tourists

  • After dark hit the GiGO gachapon floors, where hundreds of capsule machines line the walls for a few hundred yen a pull. Then wander the neon-lit main drag, which is the real Akihabara photo, and check whether a weekend fighting-game tournament is running at one of the arcades. It turns the district into an evening playground.

    Quick facts

    • Gachapon ~300-500 yen per capsule
    • Akihabara Station Electric Town exit
    • Neon peaks after dark
    • Tourneys mostly on weekends
Evening: themed cafe or Ameyoko street eats
Day4

Ikebukuro, Off the Clock

Eva Store + Sunshine City · Wed Aug 5

One district, all day, nothing booked. The official Evangelion store, the flagship anime shop, and a whole tower of stuff stacked above them — then bail out whenever you want, because tomorrow morning is the bullet train.

✦ Felix’s Remix

Eva Store + Sunshine City

Everything here is within a few blocks of Ikebukuro Station. No trains, no timed tickets, nothing to buy ahead.

  • The official Evangelion store relocated from Harajuku to the sixth floor of the Ikebukuro PARCO main building, and it is the only permanent Eva shop in the city. It runs from RADIO EVA apparel to plug-suit collabs to model kits, with in-store displays and rotating limited editions you will not find anywhere else. Walk in off the street, no timed entry.

    Quick facts

    • Ikebukuro PARCO main building, 6F
    • Open 11:00 to 21:00
    • Moved here from Harajuku
    • Limited-run goods rotate often

  • Animate's Ikebukuro flagship is the chain's flagship store and the anchor of the whole district, stacked floor over floor with manga, figures, drama CDs, and series-specific merch. The surrounding blocks are the anime quarter, so cosplayers, doujin shops, and themed cafes are all right there. Easy to lose two hours without planning any of it.

    Quick facts

    • Higashi-Ikebukuro 1-20-7
    • Weekdays 11:00 to 21:00
    • Weekends 10:00 to 20:00
    • Walking distance from PARCO

  • The observation deck on top of Sunshine 60 looks out over the whole north side of the city, and on a clear evening you get the skyline going gold and then lighting up. It costs a fraction of the famous decks, it never really sells out, and it is a lift ride from the shops rather than a train trip. Time it around sunset and stay for the switch to night.

    Quick facts

    • Open 10:00 to 21:00
    • 700 to 1,200 yen depending on the day
    • No advance ticket needed
    • Inside the Sunshine City complex

  • Sunshine Aquarium sits on the roof of the complex with an outdoor lagoon, so the sea lions swim through a tank above your head with the Tokyo skyline behind them. Downstairs the Konica Minolta planetarium runs immersive shows on a rotating schedule. Both are indoors and cold, which in an August Tokyo afternoon matters more than it sounds.

    Quick facts

    • Inside Sunshine City, rooftop level
    • Sea lion tank runs overhead
    • Planetarium in the same complex
    • Heads up: the Pokemon Center here is closed for renovation until Aug 24

  • Day 4 is the one with no ticket, no timed entry, and no reservation anywhere on it, which is deliberate. You are three heavy days in and tomorrow morning is the shinkansen to Kyoto with luggage, so the win condition here is getting back to Shinjuku with energy left. Eat well, repack, sleep. Ikebukuro back to Shinjuku is a short hop on the Yamanote line.

    Quick facts

    • Nothing on this day is pre-booked
    • Yamanote line straight back to Shinjuku
    • Thu Aug 6 is the bullet train to Kyoto
    • Hotel Gracery checkout is Aug 6
Loose day · nothing booked, nothing timed

Day5

South on the Bullet Train

Tokyo → Kyoto · Thu Aug 6

Ekiben on the platform, 2h15 at 320 km/h, Mt. Fuji out the right-side window. You land mid-day — enough for a market lunch and a first walk through the old city.

Locked inShinkansen Tokyo → Kyoto on Thu Aug 6. Solaria Nishitetsu Kyoto Premier, three nights.
✦ Felix’s Remix

Kyoto Has a Save Point Too

The bullet train is the ride, and Kyoto's nerd underbelly is the reward.

  • The bullet train from Tokyo to Kyoto runs the classic ekiben ritual: grab a station bento off the platform kiosk, then eat it at 175 mph. Book seat E (right side heading west) and Mt Fuji fills the window for a minute or two around Shin-Fuji, roughly 40 minutes out. Have the phone ready because it comes and goes fast.

    Quick facts

    • Tokyo to Kyoto ~2h15m on Nozomi
    • Ekiben ~1,000-1,500 yen
    • Seat E = right side, Fuji view
    • Reserve seats; Nozomi not on JR Pass

  • A former 1920s elementary school packed with 50,000 volumes of manga you literally pull off the wall-length shelves and read wherever you want, including on the front lawn. There are English shelves, vintage series, and rotating exhibits on the art form. Easily two hours if he falls into a series.

    Quick facts

    • Admission 900 yen
    • Near Karasuma-Oike subway station
    • Closed Wednesdays
    • Bring socks; sit-and-read all day

  • Two parallel covered shopping streets running off Nishiki Market, wall-to-wall with gachapon machines, claw-machine arcades, retro game shops, and cheap eats. It is the low-stakes place to blow a few hundred yen on UFO catchers after lunch. Fully roofed, so it works rain or shine.

    Quick facts

    • Free to walk; bring 100-yen coins
    • Off Shijo, near Nishiki Market
    • Covered arcade, weatherproof
    • Most shops open ~11am

  • Nintendo's original 1889 headquarters, from its playing-card days, now restored as a boutique hotel. You cannot tour inside without a reservation, but the preserved facade is a quick pilgrimage photo for anyone who grew up on Mario. It sits in the quiet Kagimachi area south of downtown.

    Quick facts

    • Exterior photo is free
    • Near Nintendo's old card factory district
    • Hotel/restaurant inside needs booking
    • Combine with nearby Kyoto Station
Check in · lanterns after dark
Day6

The Kyoto Postcard Day

Fushimi Inari + Higashiyama · Fri Aug 7

The two most famous sights in Japan, connected by the best old-town walk in the country. Start at sunrise to beat the heat and the crowds.

✦ Felix’s Remix

100% the Map

Completionist mode: don't quit at the viewpoint, summit the whole mountain.

  • Most people stop at the Yotsutsuji viewpoint and turn back, but the trail keeps climbing through thousands of vermilion torii gates to Ichinomine at the actual summit. The full loop is about two to three hours and thins out fast the higher you go. It is a legit hike with a real payoff and city views on the way up.

    Quick facts

    • Free, open 24 hours
    • Full summit ~2-3 hours round trip
    • JR Inari or Keihan Fushimi-Inari station
    • Wear real shoes; some stairs

  • Shops near Kiyomizu-dera rent full samurai armor or a yukata and send you out into the Higashiyama lanes for photos. The old wooden streets like Ninenzaka make an ideal backdrop. Peak main-character energy for a couple hours of walking.

    Quick facts

    • Yukata rental ~3,000-5,000 yen
    • Armor rental higher; book ahead
    • Higashiyama near Kiyomizu-dera
    • Reserve online in summer

  • The shrine never closes and the lower torii tunnels are softly lit after dark, with almost nobody around. It turns into a genuinely eerie, cinematic walk once the day crowds clear out. Bring a phone light for the upper stretches where lanterns thin out.

    Quick facts

    • Free, always open
    • Best after ~8pm for empty gates
    • Lower loop lit; upper trail dark
    • 5 min from Fushimi-Inari station

  • Hands-on dojo sessions in the Higashiyama area teach katana-drawing (iaido) basics or shuriken throwing in about an hour. An instructor walks you through stances and a few cuts, and you get to actually handle the gear. Good energy burner before dinner if the legs held up.

    Quick facts

    • ~1 hour session
    • Roughly 4,000-6,000 yen per person
    • Book ahead; English available
    • Central Kyoto / Higashiyama studios
Early start · slow afternoon
Day7

Deer, Buddha & Tea

Nara + Uji · Sat Aug 8

Japan's first capital is 45 minutes away: 1,200 wild deer that bow for crackers, and a 500-ton bronze Buddha in a colossal wooden hall. Uji, the tea capital, is on the train line home.

✦ Felix’s Remix

Deer Combat + Anime Pilgrimage

Get mobbed by bowing deer, then walk real anime locations along the river.

  • Inside Todai-ji's Great Buddha Hall, one wooden pillar has a hole cut the exact size of the giant bronze Buddha's nostril. Squeezing through it supposedly grants enlightenment, and it is an easy fit for kids but a proper wriggle for a teen. Getting half-stuck is the point; someone films it.

    Quick facts

    • Todai-ji admission 800 yen
    • In Nara Park, walkable from station
    • Hole ~30x37 cm
    • Line can form; go early

  • Uji is the real-world setting of the anime Sound! Euphonium, and you can walk the exact riverbank, the Uji-bashi bridge, and the Keihan station used as backdrops. Even if he has not seen it, the riverside temples and tea shops make Uji worth the detour. Fans photo-match scenes shot for shot.

    Quick facts

    • ~20 min by train from Kyoto
    • JR or Keihan to Uji station
    • Free riverside walk
    • Pair with Byodo-in and matcha

  • Vendors in Nara Park sell shika senbei crackers and the semi-wild deer mob you the second they see them. Hold a cracker up and many will bow their heads first, then get pushy and nip if you stall. Chaotic, hilarious, and the definitive Nara photo op.

    Quick facts

    • Deer crackers ~200 yen a stack
    • Free-roaming deer in Nara Park
    • Feed fast; they get grabby
    • Watch pockets and paper maps

  • The temple pictured on the back of the 10-yen coin, its Phoenix Hall floats over a reflecting pond and dates to 1053. Pull a 10-yen out of your pocket and match it in person before hitting Uji's matcha shops. The on-site museum holds the original phoenix statues and bell.

    Quick facts

    • Admission 700 yen (hall interior extra)
    • In Uji, near the river
    • It's on the 10-yen coin
    • Combine with a matcha stop
Bow to a deer — it bows back
Day8

Bamboo, Gold, then Osaka

Arashiyama + Kinkaku-ji · Sun Aug 9

Kyoto's grand finale in one sweep — bamboo forest early, the Golden Pavilion at midday — then a 15-minute bullet-train hop and you're in Osaka by dinner.

Locked inTrain Kyoto → Osaka on Sun Aug 9. Fairfield by Marriott Namba, four nights.
✦ Felix’s Remix

From Bamboo to Neon (Yakuza IRL)

Monkeys and moss in the morning, then step straight into a video-game city at night.

  • A 20-minute uphill hike above Arashiyama lands you at a hilltop clearing full of wild Japanese macaques and a panoramic view over Kyoto. You feed them from inside a mesh hut, so you are the one caged while they roam free. Real wild monkeys, no fences between you on the trail.

    Quick facts

    • Admission 600 yen
    • 20 min hike up
    • Near Arashiyama Togetsukyo bridge
    • Feed only from inside the hut

  • Dotonbori is the real-world basis for Sotenbori in the Yakuza games, so run the first Osaka night as a walking tour: find the Glico running man sign, the giant moving Kani Doraku crab, and the Ebisu-bashi bridge. Neon overload, canal reflections, and street food everywhere. Fans clock the geography instantly.

    Quick facts

    • Free to walk
    • Namba station, then walk to canal
    • Best after dark for neon
    • Try takoyaki and kushikatsu

  • Renting e-bikes lets you hit the bamboo grove, Togetsukyo bridge, and the quieter moss temples in one loop while beating the tour buses. The electric assist makes the low hills nothing. Best done early when the bamboo path is still empty.

    Quick facts

    • E-bike rental ~1,500-2,500 yen/day
    • Rent near Arashiyama station
    • Go early to beat crowds
    • Covers grove, bridge, temples

  • An oval-shaped Ferris wheel built into the Don Quijote store right on the Dotonbori canal, unusual because it moves sideways as well as up. It gives a quick neon overview of Minami before the takoyaki crawl. Short ride, big first-night payoff over the canal.

    Quick facts

    • Ride ~800 yen
    • At Don Quijote Dotonbori
    • Oval track, sideways motion
    • Great canal night views
Kyoto out · Osaka in
Day9

Osaka, Player Two

Den Den Town + Dotonbori · Mon Aug 10

Osaka's answer to Akihabara — cheaper, less touristy, deeper bargain bins — plus the loudest food street in Japan and an eight-floor Round1 arcade to close the night.

✦ Felix’s Remix

Den Den Town Boss Rush

Osaka's Akihabara, but grittier and cheaper - retro-hunt all day, arcade all night, no filler.

  • Super Potato is the legendary retro-game chain, stacked floor to ceiling with boxed Famicom carts, loose Game Boy games, old controllers, and a retro arcade corner. The surrounding Nipponbashi strip, aka Den Den Town, is Osaka's answer to Akihabara but cheaper and less picked-over. It is a genuine hunting ground for CRT-era gaming loot rather than a tourist trap.

    Quick facts

    • Nipponbashi is Osaka's electronics district
    • Nearest stop: Nipponbashi or Ebisucho Station
    • Bring cash, small shops prefer it
    • Cheaper than Tokyo's Akihabara

  • Round1 Stadium in Namba is a multi-floor entertainment tower with UFO catcher claw machines, rhythm and fighting arcades, bowling, karaoke, and darts. The VS Park floor is Bandai Namco's active-play zone with full-body sports mini-games like sprinting against a virtual cheetah. It is built for a head-to-head dad-versus-son night that runs from claw machines to a rhythm-game showdown.

    Quick facts

    • Located in Namba entertainment district
    • VS Park is a timed-entry ticket
    • Bowling, arcade, karaoke under one roof
    • Open late into the night

  • Pokemon Center Osaka sits inside Daimaru Umeda and carries region-exclusive plush and merch you cannot get abroad. Nearby the Shonen Jump flagship stocks One Piece, Dragon Ball, and Jujutsu Kaisen goods, while Mandarake Grand Chaos runs multiple floors of vintage figures, doujin, and out-of-print collectibles. Together they are the anime-merch trifecta of the Umeda district.

    Quick facts

    • Pokemon Center inside Daimaru Umeda, 13th floor
    • All three near Umeda / Osaka Station
    • Mandarake is best for vintage figures
    • Osaka-exclusive Pokemon merch here

  • Kuromon Ichiba is a covered market where stalls grill scallops, tuna, and wagyu skewers you eat on the spot. Do it fast, then hit a gachapon hall in Den Den Town where a wall of capsule-toy machines runs a few hundred yen a pull. It is the perfect low-stakes souvenir sweep: cheap, random, and endlessly re-playable.

    Quick facts

    • Kuromon near Nipponbashi Station
    • Grilled skewers roughly 500 to 1,500 yen
    • Gachapon pulls approx 300 to 500 yen
    • Bring 100-yen coins for machines
Arcade + food day · Felix's Osaka
Day10

The Big One

Super Nintendo World · Tue Aug 11
DEFINITE YES

Walk through a warp pipe and come out inside a life-size Mushroom Kingdom. Full day at Universal on Tuesday Aug 11. Studio Passes and Express Pass 5 are bought and in hand, so the queues are solved — the only thing that matters now is being at the Nintendo World gate at 12:30 sharp.

Locked inStudio Pass + Express Pass 5, both bought. Nintendo World entry 12:30 PM, Mario Kart 12:30, Yoshi 1:00. Print the QR codes.
✦ Felix’s Remix

Two Laps of the Mushroom Kingdom

SNW is already the dream day - so squeeze every drop out of it and don't let the park close the fun.

  • Bought on Klook for Tue Aug 11: two 1-Day Studio Passes plus two Universal Express Pass 5 ~Adventure Special~. That covers Mario Kart, Yoshi's Adventure, The Flying Dinosaur, Hollywood Dream and Minion Mayhem, with guaranteed Super Nintendo World entry at 12:30. Standby lines run 90 to 180 minutes in August, so this is the whole ballgame. Once you're inside Nintendo World you can stay as long as you want, but there is no re-entry if you walk out.

    Quick facts

    • Nintendo World entry 12:30–1:30 PM
    • Mario Kart 12:30 · Yoshi 1:00
    • Mine-Cart Madness is standby only
    • Print the QR codes, screenshots refused

  • This zone opened December 2024 and roughly doubled the size of Super Nintendo World with jungle theming, Kong statues, and hidden interactive gimmicks. The star is Mine-Cart Madness, a coaster that fakes a gap in the track so your cart looks like it jumps a broken rail. Early in the day the DK side runs shorter waits than the Mario area, so hit it first then loop back.

    Quick facts

    • Opened December 2024, newest USJ zone
    • Mine-Cart Madness is the signature coaster
    • Do DK side first, shorter morning lines
    • Inside the SNW timed-entry area

  • The Power-Up Band is a wristband that syncs to your USJ app and tracks coins and stamps as you punch question blocks and beat mini-games scattered around the land. Collect three keys from the mini-boss challenges and you unlock the final Bowser's Challenge, a competitive score battle. It turns the whole area into a dad-versus-son leaderboard, and Mario Kart: Bowser's Challenge itself uses AR headsets to steer and throw shells.

    Quick facts

    • Power-Up Band approx 3,800 yen each
    • Band needed to save mini-game scores
    • Three keys unlock the boss battle
    • Comes in Mario, Luigi, Peach and more

  • After sunset Super Nintendo World lights up neon and the whole Mario area glows, which is the best time for photos and short waits. Hollywood Dream is a smooth backwards-and-forwards launch coaster and Flying Dinosaur is a flying coaster that dangles you face-down over the park. Close the night with a train to Dotonbori for late takoyaki under the Glico running-man sign.

    Quick facts

    • SNW neon peaks after dark
    • Flying Dinosaur is a face-down flying coaster
    • USJ to Dotonbori approx 20 minutes by train
    • Late rides often have shortest waits
Tickets bought · SNW entry 12:30 PM
Day11

The Hiroshima Day

Peace Park + Miyajima · Wed Aug 12

Ninety minutes west on the bullet train for the day people most often call the best of their trip. Heavy in the morning, beautiful in the afternoon.

Castle or Bust: The Flex Day Fork

Hiroshima is heavy and far - either commit to it with the fun Miyajima half, or bail and go full samurai-and-neon instead.

  • Spend the morning at the Peace Memorial Park and Museum, then ferry out to Miyajima to see the great torii gate that appears to float at high tide. The island has free-roaming bowing deer and the Mount Misen ropeway climbs to viewpoints over the Seto Inland Sea. Leading with the heavy history and finishing on the island keeps the day from ending on a somber note.

    Quick facts

    • Osaka to Hiroshima approx 1.5 hrs shinkansen
    • Miyajima ferry approx 10 minutes each way
    • Misen ropeway approx 2,000 yen round trip
    • Check tide times for the floating torii

  • Himeji sits directly on the Osaka-Hiroshima shinkansen line and is Japan's largest surviving original castle, never destroyed and never rebuilt in concrete. Its white keep and maze of defensive gates are the real-deal feudal fortress that a hundred anime and samurai films have copied. You can climb the original wooden interior all the way to the top floor.

    Quick facts

    • Osaka to Himeji approx 30-45 min shinkansen
    • Castle a 15-minute walk from station
    • Entry approx 1,000 yen
    • UNESCO site, Japan's biggest original keep

  • Skip the trains and stay local in Shinsekai, a retro district of neon backstreets built around the Tsutenkaku tower and packed with kushikatsu deep-fried skewer joints. Tsutenkaku has an observation deck and a spiral slide that drops you down the tower. Next door, Spa World is a giant bathhouse with European and Asian themed onsen floors for a father-son soak.

    Quick facts

    • Shinsekai is Osaka's retro neon district
    • Tsutenkaku deck approx 1,000 yen
    • Spa World day pass approx 1,500 yen
    • Kushikatsu: no double-dipping the sauce

  • If Super Nintendo World left you wanting more, USJ sells discounted twilight and after-4pm tickets that get you back in for the evening. Lines drop sharply late in the day, so you can mop up the coasters and rides you missed the first time with near-zero waits. It is the cheapest way to squeeze a second park visit into the trip.

    Quick facts

    • Twilight tickets cheaper than full day
    • Lines thin out after 4pm
    • SNW may still need timed entry
    • Check park closing time first

Last Boss: Osaka Castle, Then Loot the Store

One iconic morning view, then a fast souvenir-and-gachapon sweep before wheels-up.

  • Osaka Castle's gold-trimmed keep rises over a wide moat and stone walls, and you can circle the grounds and get the money shot from the plaza in well under an hour. The Miraiza building at the base has restaurants, souvenirs, and free samurai-armor photo spots. Skip the museum line inside unless the flight is late and you want the elevator to the top deck.

    Quick facts

    • Grounds free, castle interior approx 600 yen
    • Nearest: Osakajokoen or Tanimachi 4-chome
    • Exterior loop under one hour
    • Miraiza has samurai armor photo ops

  • Make the last meal count with a final round of street food: takoyaki, fresh uni and scallops at Kuromon, or a farewell bowl of tonkotsu ramen in Dotonbori. Both spots let you graze stall to stall so you can hit the exact things you will crave on the flight home. Go early before the lunch crowds fill the arcades.

    Quick facts

    • Kuromon opens around 9am
    • Takoyaki approx 500-700 yen per tray
    • Dotonbori is a 10-min walk from Namba
    • Cash speeds up stall ordering

  • Don Quijote in Dotonbori is a chaotic multi-floor discount store crammed with snacks, exclusive Kit-Kat flavors, cosmetics, gadgets, and last-minute souvenirs. Bolted to the building is the Ebisu Tower, an oval-shaped ferris wheel that gives one final ride over the neon canal. It is the ultimate one-stop souvenir dump before the airport.

    Quick facts

    • Open very late, good for last-minute runs
    • Ebisu Tower wheel approx 800 yen
    • Tax-free counter for tourist purchases
    • Right on the Dotonbori canal

  • Blow the last of your loose yen coins on a wall of gachapon capsule machines, then swing by Pokemon Center Osaka in Daimaru Umeda for the region-exclusive merch you can only get here. It is a low-stakes, high-fun closer that turns leftover change into souvenirs. Time it so you finish with enough buffer to reach the airport.

    Quick facts

    • Pokemon Center in Daimaru Umeda
    • Gachapon pulls approx 300-500 yen
    • Grab Osaka-exclusive Pokemon goods
    • Save yen coins for the machines
