スイカSuica & Money

Top the Suica up properly
¥1,000 is about five train rides. A single Tokyo hop runs ¥180 to ¥210, and Suica also pays for convenience stores, vending machines, and plenty of restaurants. Load ¥10,000 before you land, then top up as you go. Use the Mastercard or the Amex, because a foreign-issued Visa gets rejected for Suica top-ups. Cash works at any 7-Eleven register.
Put a Suica on FD's phone
Wallet, plus button, Transit Card, Japan, Suica. His own phone, his own card. No deposit. Don't let him be the guy at the ticket machine every ride.
Tell the card issuers you're gone Aug 1 to 21
Cover Japan and Cayman in one call, and confirm which card in your wallet has no foreign transaction fee. That's the one that lives in the front slot.
Yen cash for arrival day
Japan is far more cashless than it was, but small ramen counters, shrines, and Nara deer crackers still want coins. 7-Eleven ATMs at the airport give the best rate, so don't buy yen here.
